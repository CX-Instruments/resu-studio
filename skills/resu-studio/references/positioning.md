# Positioning before rewriting

Read this with `writing-engine.md` and the saved application brief before `rewriting.md`.
Credible conviction is the default mode, not a restriction on personal modes. Every
mode shares the evidence rules below, ATS readability, human engagement and tailoring.

## Four pillars, extracted internally

Do not print Passion, Strengths or UVP as CV headings. Mark a pillar absent when its
evidence is missing. A compelling phrase does not fill an evidence gap.

**Passion is demonstrated choice.** Look for repeated, self-initiated work beyond the
assigned incident: a procedure written, a team trained, a recurring failure fixed.
Duties alone do not establish motivation. A stated future direction is a targeting
signal, never experience. Express supported passion as a working habit in the profile
(often its third sentence) and one bullet showing the move from incident to system.
Do not force a third sentence or write “passionate about”.

**Strengths are capabilities this employer needs and the history contains.** Intersect
the essential criteria and relevant responsibilities with actual duties and outcomes.
Each named strength must have a supporting bullet. Use equivalent employer language in
skills and strong bullet openings; do not upgrade similar work into sector expertise.

**Impact is observable evidence.** Use raw figures, before/after, scale, frequency,
artefacts or documented changes near the action. Preserve estimates and ownership.
Prefer “about six a month to one” to a newly calculated percentage. Never infer causal
credit from sequence alone. One useful scale fact can sit in a role's first bullet or
scope line. An honest qualitative outcome is sufficient when no figure exists.

**Value is a supported pattern that makes the candidate distinguishable.** Identify
how their contributions connect across the available evidence, without claiming they
are objectively unique or better than other applicants. Recast the pattern once in the
profile, prove it in the strongest bullet and echo it lightly where another example
adds evidence. The hospitality fixture's process/documentation/training pattern is an
example from that person's history, never a template to impose on another candidate.

Harvard/Yale sentence principles remain the execution rules: specific, active,
fact-based, scannable, express rather than impress, no personal pronouns on the CV.
They do not replace evidence selection or positioning. Warmth comes from concrete
beneficiaries, relationships and natural cadence, not invented feelings or traits.

## North star

**Rewrite toward credible conviction.** Make the person's strongest relevant
contribution easy to see, in language they could say aloud and evidence they can
defend. Show what they are trusted to do, how they do it and why it matters. Use the
employer's terms where the record supports them. Prefer concrete work, context and
proof over praise. Never force a metric, invent passion, announce a personal brand or
make the person sound like the advertisement.

## Set the case before editing lines

In five working lines, identify:

- the target and what it values
- two to four strongest relevant assets or repeated strengths
- the facts that prove each one
- the work they said they want more of
- the voice already established by their CV

If a strength or motivation has no proof, ask one useful question or leave it out.

## Select, then write

- Build one coherent case; do not polish every fact and keep them all.
- Choose the strongest relevant evidence. Differentiate through detail, not adjectives.
- A bullet should show the person's contribution and its project, problem or context;
  add the result or value where it is known.
- Quantify or qualify when supported. Otherwise show truthful scale, difficulty,
  responsibility, standard or beneficiary.
- Make the person's part in a team result clear.
- Integrate supported employer terms naturally where a reader and keyword search can
  find them.
- Read the whole CV: the first page makes the case and later lines prove it.

The result is specific, active, clear, direct, fact-based and easy to scan. It applies
Harvard's strongest-assets and differentiation guidance, and Yale's contribution,
project or problem, result and evidence guidance, without becoming salesy.
